# When one brain should become two

**Split by subject, not by job title.** Research, a business and your own personal matters are different subjects with different sources, different readers and different risks. Each deserves its own brain with its own edges. An agent then goes only to the brain its task needs.

**Why not one brain for everything?** The gate's first question, "is it on this subject?", only works when the subject has edges. A brief per subject stays short enough to read properly. And an agent that reaches only the brain its job needs cannot carry your personal notes into a work task.

**Signs it is time to split**
- The gate in `CLAUDE.md` needs "except" clauses to keep material out.
- The weekly brief gets too long to read properly.
- Answers start mixing material that should never meet (personal and work, one client and another).
- One part of the brain needs stricter privacy than the rest.

**When a job spans two brains**
The cost of splitting is that a pattern from one field may never reach a problem in another. So for a job that needs both:
- point the agent at both brains in its instructions (two read-first blocks);
- ask it to say which brain each point came from;
- save any new connection it finds to the `intake/` of the brain that will use it, so it passes that brain's gate at your next review.

**One agent is usually enough.** One capable agent reading the right brain does most work well, and nothing gets lost between hands. It costs more on a strong model. A structure of specialist agents pays off only when something reliable carries the context and the timing between them, and when you can see each hand-over happen. Without that, the agent at the top ends up doing the work itself.
