# The feed gate

Everything new lands in `intake/` first, except sources you add to `raw/` yourself; those, too, reach a page only through the weekly brief and your yes. Nothing goes straight into `pages/`.

## Four kinds of feed
1. **Scheduled scans.** A daily or weekly search for new material on the subject (news, research, reports), saved into `intake/` with its link and date.
2. **Your own drops.** Copy-paste, screenshots, meeting notes, a voice memo you transcribed, an answer another agent saved. Save them into `intake/` as they come.
3. **A library.** Articles, papers, reports and your own past work. Put them in `raw/` once; add more as you find them.
4. **A map of the field.** If available, every few months, a screened scan of a whole research database on the subject, written as one page marked abstract only. It gives breadth, not evidence. See `research-funnel.md` in the kit.

## What may enter (all must hold)
- **On the subject**, inside the edges written in `CLAUDE.md`.
- **Checkable.** It has a link or a file you can open again, and a date.
- **Still useful in a year.** An idea, a method or evidence, not a passing event.
- **New to the brain.** If the brain already holds the same claim, add one line to the existing page, not a new page.

## What stays out
- Anything that fails the four tests above. It can still be useful today; it just does not go into the brain.
- Material your employer has not cleared, other people's personal data, passwords and keys.

## Where the decision is made
The weekly brief proposes. You decide. The brain changes only when you say yes.
