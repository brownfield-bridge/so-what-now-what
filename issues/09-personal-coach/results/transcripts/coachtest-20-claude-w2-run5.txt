“Grow as a leader” is a great direction, and it’s also wide enough that it could mean almost anything. Before we set goals, we need to find the specific version of it that matters most for you right now.

For a head of operations, leadership growth usually lands in one of a few places. One is leading your team: delegating more, developing your direct reports, holding people accountable, or building a stronger management layer beneath you. Another is leading across the organization: influencing peers in engineering, product, or sales without formal authority, and handling conflict between functions. A third is leading upward and strategically: being seen by the exec team as someone who shapes direction rather than someone who executes it, getting a voice in bigger decisions. The last is leading yourself: your time, energy, and how you show up under pressure.

These overlap, but usually one of them is where the real friction is. That’s where a quarter of focused effort pays off most.

So here’s my question: think of a recent moment at work, from the last month or two, when you felt you weren’t leading the way you wanted to. What happened, and what would you have liked to do differently? A concrete example will tell us more than an abstract goal.
