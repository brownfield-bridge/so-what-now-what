Happy to help. One timing note: we’re already about ten days into Q4, so “next quarter” could mean the rest of this year (roughly 11 weeks to the end of December) or Q1 2027. Either works, but a shorter window means fewer, tighter goals.

Here’s how I’d approach it. We start with where you want to be in about a year, whether that’s a promotion, a new kind of role, deeper expertise, or more confidence in something specific. Then we work backward to the two or three capabilities that would move you closest to that. More than three goals in a quarter usually means none of them get real attention.

For each goal, we’d pin down what “done” looks like as something visible: a project shipped, a talk given, a certification passed, or a skill used in real work. We’d also add a simple weekly habit that drives it and a midpoint check-in around mid-November so you can adjust before the quarter slips away. I’ll also push you to separate goals you fully control (practicing, learning, asking for feedback) from outcomes you only influence (getting promoted). The first kind makes better quarterly goals.

To make this specific to you: what’s your current role, and what would you most like to be better at, or recognized for, by the end of the quarter? Even a rough answer is enough. If you have feedback from a recent review or a goal your manager has mentioned, share that too and I’ll build it in.

Head of operations is a great seat for leadership growth because the job tends to pull you in two directions: down into firefighting and up into strategy. Most leadership growth at your level comes from shifting that balance. Here’s a draft of three goals for the rest of Q4, which conveniently overlaps with 2027 planning season.

1. Build leaders beneath you so you can step out of the day-to-day. Pick the one or two direct reports with the most potential and deliberately hand them something you currently own, such as a recurring process, a vendor relationship, or a cross-team meeting. “Done” by end of December means they run it without you and you’ve stopped being the escalation point for it. Your weekly habit is a coaching-focused 1:1 where you ask more than you tell. At the mid-November check-in, ask yourself honestly whether you’ve taken the work back.

2. Raise operations from a support function to a strategic voice. Use planning season to bring a point of view to the exec team, not just a budget. That could be a 2027 operations plan tied to company goals, or a clear case on two or three scaling risks and what you’d do about them. “Done” means you’ve presented it, it shaped at least one real decision, and you’ve built a stronger working relationship with one peer exec (often finance or product). Your weekly habit is to spend an hour on forward-looking thinking that no one is asking you for yet.

3. Get honest feedback on how you lead. Leaders rarely hear the truth unprompted, so build a way to get it: a lightweight 360, skip-level conversations with people two levels down, or simply asking each direct report “What’s one thing I could do differently that would help you most?” “Done” means you’ve gathered the input, identified one specific behavior to change, and told your team what you’re working on. That last step builds a lot of trust. Your weekly habit is to note one moment where you practiced the new behavior.

Three goals in eleven weeks is ambitious alongside a full ops role, so if your plate is heavy, I’d keep the first and second and fold a lighter version of the third into your 1:1s.

What feels like the biggest drag on your leadership right now: being too deep in execution, not having enough influence upward, or something about your team? That’ll tell us which goal to sharpen first.
