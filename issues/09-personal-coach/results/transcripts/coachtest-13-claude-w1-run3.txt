Happy to help. Since we’re in October, I’ll assume you mean the quarter running through the end of December. Shout if you’re actually planning for January to March.

Here’s a structure that tends to work well for a three-month horizon:

Look back first. Spend a few minutes on what went well last quarter, what stalled, and why. Goals that ignore the last quarter’s patterns usually repeat them.
Pick two or three themes. Twelve weeks is short, and it disappears fast once holidays arrive in December. A small number of focus areas beats a long list you’ll abandon by November.
Make each goal concrete. “Get better at public speaking” is hard to act on. “Give two internal talks and get feedback after each” isn’t. Aim for something you could clearly say yes or no to on December 31st.
Separate outcomes from habits. Pair each goal with a weekly behavior you control, like an hour of focused study on Tuesdays or one stakeholder coffee per week. Outcomes motivate, but habits are what you can actually manage.
Set a mid-point check-in. Mid-November is a good moment to ask what’s working, what’s slipping, and whether anything should be dropped.

To make the goals fit you, it would help to know what you have in mind. Is this about your career (a promotion, a new skill, a role change), technical or coding skills, or personal growth? Tell me a bit about where you are now and what you’d like to be true by year-end, and we can draft the actual goals together.

That’s a great role to be growing in, because ops leaders often get pulled into firefighting, which crowds out the leadership work that actually scales. Q4 is also a useful quarter for this, since it usually brings 2027 planning, budgeting, and year-end reviews, all natural places to practice leadership skills.

Here’s a draft built around three themes common for heads of operations. Treat it as a starting point to react to, not a final plan.

1. Develop your team so the function runs without you

The clearest sign of an ops leader’s maturity is how much keeps working when they step back.

Goal: Identify one process or decision area you personally own today and fully hand it off to a direct report by mid-December, with them running it and you only reviewing results.
Goal: Have a career development conversation with every direct report this quarter, separate from performance reviews, and agree on one growth goal for each of them.
Weekly habit: Coaching-style 1:1s where you ask more than you tell. A useful check is whether your report did most of the talking.

2. Shift from executing to shaping strategy

Many ops heads are seen as excellent at making things run but less often as strategic partners to the CEO and other department heads.

Goal: Bring a proactive 2027 operations proposal to the leadership team during planning season, framed around business outcomes (growth, margin, customer experience) rather than ops metrics alone.
Goal: Build stronger relationships with two peers outside your usual orbit, such as product, sales, or finance, by understanding their biggest 2027 challenges and how ops could help.
Weekly habit: Protect a two-hour block for thinking and planning work that no operational issue can take.

3. Understand how you’re actually showing up

Leadership growth is hard to steer without honest input.

Goal: Gather structured feedback by the end of October, either a lightweight 360 or direct questions to your manager, two peers, and two reports, such as “What should I start, stop, and continue doing?”
Goal: Pick one specific behavior from that feedback to work on deliberately, then check back with the same people in December on whether they noticed a change.
Weekly habit: A 15-minute Friday reflection on where your time went, what you’d handle differently, and what you avoided.

One caution: running all three themes at full intensity alongside a busy Q4 is a lot. If your plate is full, choose the two that matter most and keep the third light.

To sharpen these into goals that fit your situation, it would help to know what feels like the biggest gap right now. Is it being too deep in the weeds, team capability, influence with leadership, or something else entirely?
