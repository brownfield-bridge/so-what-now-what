Those two worries fit together better than you might think. Not knowing why his work has slipped actually gives you a better way into the conversation. You don’t have to show up with a verdict. Goal 2: Make feedback a normal habit across the team.
Hold regular one-on-ones with each team member, every week or every other week. In each one, give at least one piece of specific feedback, positive or constructive, and give everyone at least one piece of constructive feedback per month. Keep a simple note for each person so you can see whether you’re really doing it or quietly avoiding some people. Success by January: no one on your team is surprised by how you see their work, and giving criticism feels routine rather than dreaded.

Goal 3 (optional): Get feedback on your own leadership.
By mid-November, ask your manager, and ideally one or two team members, what you could do better as a lead. Act on at least one thing they say. Asking for feedback yourself makes it much easier to give it, and it shows your team that feedback runs both ways.
