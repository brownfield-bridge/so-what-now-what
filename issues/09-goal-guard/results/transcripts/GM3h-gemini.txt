You are completely right. When you are making eye contact with someone in a serious conversation, looking down at your notes can feel like breaking the connection, so the notes end up ignored.

Let's solve this by stealing a standard engineering practice: pairing. When you review code or an architecture diagram with a colleague, you don't stare at each other—you both look at the screen. It takes the pressure off the interpersonal dynamic and focuses both of you on the work.

What if you treat your summary exactly like that? Instead of keeping your notes hidden, you intentionally share them. If you are remote, you share your screen. If you are in person, you physically angle your laptop or slide your notepad between the two of you.

By saying, "Here is what I wrote down," and putting it right in their line of sight, you are forced to look at the page, and the intense, face-to-face eye contact is naturally broken. The feedback becomes a shared artifact you are both looking at together.
