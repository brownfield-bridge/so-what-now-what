Your AI pilots are not necessarily wrong. Your org chart might be.

List your AI initiatives, the live ones and the ones you are only weighing. Answer four questions about each. This tool sorts which are real decisions and which are purchases wearing a pilot's badge, then names the one to fix this week.

The rows below are a worked example. Edit them, or clear and enter your own. It is not a readiness score; it classifies each initiative one at a time. Nothing is stored or sent anywhere; it all runs in your browser. When you are done, copy the memo or save the page as a PDF.

Your portfolio

Org-design decision = business owner + a process (and the skill it demands) that changes + an autonomy boundary + your own success test.
How the classification works (the four questions)

Each initiative is scored on four org-design conditions. The one that stays empty is the decision that was never made.

  1. Owner. A business owner whose own number moves when this works, not a systems owner and not nobody.
  2. Process (and skill) change. A named way of working changes when it succeeds, and it demands a new skill someone actually builds. If nothing changes, it's a purchase.
  3. Autonomy boundary. You've decided what the system may do alone and what needs sign-off.
  4. Own success test. You judge it on a test you wrote, not the vendor's benchmark.

All four → Org-design decision. These produce operating change. One to three → Decision half-made; the missing column is your next move. None, with no owner and no real use → IT choice (theater). None → IT choice: fine as IT, just not a transformation.

Live vs. a bet you are weighing. For a live initiative, the tool flags the widest gap between what it costs and what it has changed. For a bet you have not started, it classifies the shape it is heading toward on current plans, so you can reshape it before you fund it, not after.

The tool then flags the one initiative with the widest cost-versus-change gap. That is this week's work.
