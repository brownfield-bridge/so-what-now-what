# Start here: build your context base, step by step

A complete walkthrough. About 30 minutes to a working base, then 20 minutes a week to keep it. No vector database, no platform, no code required.

## What you'll get

- A working base you can stand up in about 30 minutes
- A way to ask real questions and get sourced, cited answers
- A 20-minute weekly practice that keeps it honest
- A clonable starter with a worked example inside

## What you need

- An AI you already use: Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Cowork.
- A folder on your own drive. That is the whole stack.

## Step 1. Stand up the base (about 20 minutes)

Pick one of two ways.

**A. From the prompt (fastest from a blank page).** Open `setup-prompt.txt`, paste the whole thing into your AI, and fill the four bracketed lines. It will propose your folders, write your root map (`CONTEXT.md`), and give you a retrieval prompt. Create those folders on your drive and save the `CONTEXT.md` at the root.

**B. From the scaffold (start from a finished example).** Copy the `starter/` folder to your drive and rename it to your area. Open `CONTEXT.md` and edit the bracketed lines to fit you. The `starter/` already contains a worked example you can read, then replace: a decision, an assumption, and a signal that contradicts it.

## Step 2. Put one real thing in (about 5 minutes)

Add one real file to `decisions/` (or your equivalent), in the shape shown in `starter/decisions/2025-09-exit-public-tender-practice.md`: the call, the options, the why, and what it assumed. This one file sets the pattern, and the rest are easy.

## Step 3. Ask it something real (about 2 minutes)

Open a session with your AI, paste `retrieval-prompt.txt`, add a real question at the end, and run it. Watch it read `CONTEXT.md`, walk to the right folder, and name the file it answered from. If the base does not hold the answer, it should say so rather than guess. That is the base working.

## Step 4. Feed it (ongoing, seconds at a time)

When new material arrives, a board deck, a brief, a note, drop it into `inbox/`, named and dated. Do not stop to file it perfectly; the weekly review does that.

## Step 5. Maintain it (20 minutes a week, the part that lasts)

Once a week, at a day and time you choose, run the upkeep:

- **In Claude or Cowork:** add `weekly-maintenance.skill.md` as a skill and say "run my weekly context-base review."
- **Anywhere else:** paste `weekly-maintenance-prompt.txt` into a session pointed at your base.

It hands you a checklist (move this, cut that, this assumption is now contradicted by that signal). You approve each item. Nothing changes until you say yes. This is the move everyone skips and the one that decides whether the base is an asset in a year or a graveyard.

## Step 6. Automate the reminder (optional)

If you would rather the review showed up on its own, see `scheduled-review.md`. It still proposes a checklist for you to approve; it never edits your files on its own.

## The four moves, in one line

**Shape** the folders, **Anchor** the root map, **Feed** the inbox, **Maintain** weekly. The first three take an afternoon. The fourth is the practice.

## Files in this kit

- `GUIDE.md` (this file) and `README.md` (the why)
- `setup-prompt.txt` / `setup-prompt.md` and `context-base.skill.md` (build it)
- `retrieval-prompt.txt` (ask it)
- `starter/` (a finished, worked example you clone)
- `weekly-maintenance.skill.md` / `weekly-maintenance-prompt.txt` and `scheduled-review.md` (keep it)
- `visuals/` (the one-pager and a sample of a base)

## When not to build this

If you already have thousands of users needing audited access control, buy the platform. If your material changes hourly and you want the search everyone else already runs, buy the platform. A context base is for one person, one team, or one project reasoning against private material at human tempo. Below that scale, build. Above it, buy.
