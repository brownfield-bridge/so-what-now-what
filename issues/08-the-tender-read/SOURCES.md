# Sources

## Read at primary

**Peter Kraljic**, "Purchasing Must Become Supply Management", *Harvard Business Review*, September-October 1983, pp. 110-117. Read at primary 27 September 2026.
Used for: the classification of purchases by profit impact and supply risk; bottleneck and strategic items; contingency as a defining task for bottleneck items. Kraljic wrote about materials and components; applying the logic to software is a transfer, which the article allows: no list of evaluation criteria is equally applicable to every industry.

**Michael R. Wade, with Andrew Tarling**, "The Digital Business Agility Imperative: How companies can fight digital disruption", IMD *Tomorrow's Challenges*, 2016. Read at primary 29 September 2026, all five pages.
Used for: the three pillars of digital business agility, *"hyperawareness, informed decision-making, and fast execution"*, which *"are not technologies, but rather technology-enabled capabilities"* and *"build upon one another"*; and the failure the checking step addresses, companies that fail *"despite having the information they need to make the right moves"* because *"assumptions held by top management were not tested or questioned"*. Wade and Tarling wrote about whole companies; applying the pillars to one person with a signature is a transfer. The same model appears in *Digital Vortex* (Loucks, Macaulay, Noronha and Wade, 2016), which has not been read.

## Credited, not claimed

**Obligation and term** are ordinary contract practice: the distinction between a promise to perform and a provision that governs. **Declared exclusions** are standard bid language. What this method adds is keeping explicit refusals separate from non-answers, and counting each.
