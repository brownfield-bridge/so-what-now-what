Before anything else

Is the machine doing any of these?

This comes before the make, before the fault, before the quote. If any of it is true, there is no arithmetic to do.

  • A burning smell, or a smell of hot plastic
  • Scorch marks, browning or melting around the plug, socket or controls
  • A smell of gas anywhere near it
  • Anyone has felt a shock, a tingle or a buzz from the case, the door or the pipework
  • Water is reaching the socket, the cable, or the electrics inside

Then stop here. This is not a money question.

Switch it off at the socket and pull the plug. If you cannot reach the plug, turn it off at the consumer unit. If there is any smell of gas, do not touch a switch — open the windows, leave, and ring the gas emergency number from outside.

Get a qualified engineer to look at it before anyone uses it again. Do not run it once more to see whether it does it again.

This tool will not weigh a repair against a replacement while any of that is true, because the answer would not depend on the money.

Close the loop

What have you decided?

A job book grows the only way a job book can: someone comes back and says what happened. Answer this, and the next person asking about the same make, the same fault, at the same sort of age gets a slightly better reading than you got. One row per person who used it. That is the whole mechanism, and it is why there is no dataset here that anybody bought.

What it asked you for, and what it will never ask

Two fields, and that is all of it: what you decided, and what it cost. No account, no email, no address, no name. The tool computes on what you type and collects nothing else.

That decision has a price, and it is worth naming rather than quietly enjoying. The reading above tells you how many machines like yours went twelve months without the fault coming back. A book can only learn that by asking you again in a year — and asking you again in a year means keeping a way to reach you.

What it can learn from you today
What you decided, and what it actually came to. Those land in the cost range and the follow–on figure the next person reads, straight away.
What it cannot learn, and will not
Whether your repair held. That column fills only from machines this repairer happens to see again, never from you — because the tool has chosen not to hold anything that could reach you in a year.

It is a real trade, and it probably goes the way you would want it to go. But it is not free, and the twelve–month column is exactly where the cost lands.

What these records are

Every job in here is invented for the demonstration. The makes are invented too — no real manufacturer appears anywhere in this tool.

They stand for the job book of one repair business in one town. Not a survey, not an industry dataset, not your town.

What the comparison leaves out

  • Your machine's own history, which the records do not have
  • How hard it has been used, and by how many people
  • Whether this particular engineer is any good
  • What days without a machine cost you
  • What the old one is worth sold or scrapped
  • What the old one draws in electricity, which is unknown

Before you agree to the job

Three questions, all about the transaction rather than the machine. This tool has no view on how appliances behave, so it will not tell you which parts are cheap or which make lasts.

  • Is the call–out fee credited against the repair if I go ahead?
  • Does the repair carry twelve months on parts and labour?
  • Is the part still manufactured?
