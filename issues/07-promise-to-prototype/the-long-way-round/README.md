# The long way round

**This folder is a record, not a template. Nothing in it is offered as a thing to copy.**

Before either of the prototypes in this kit existed, the promise went through an eighteen-question interview and came out as a 190-line brief. That brief went straight to a builder, and the build that came back is here too.

It was not a bad brief. It scored four out of six, it survived two days of work, and it produced the refusals that make build 2 worth looking at.

**What was wrong was the order.** Writing it first meant spending two days deciding things that an afternoon's free prototype would have decided better - and three of them it got flatly wrong, in ways nothing showed up until something had to render on a screen.

## What is here

**[the-first-brief.md](the-first-brief.md)** - the brief as it stood before anything was built, with its four-of-six score and both gaps published.

**[build-2026-09-11.html](build-2026-09-11.html)** - what came out of it.

## Why keep it at all

The issue claims that two days of interviewing missed things that one sentence found in an afternoon. **A reader who wants to check that claim needs to see the two days.**

Deleting the evidence for your own least flattering finding is exactly the move this newsletter would call out in somebody else.

## What the build found wrong with the brief, within an hour

- It promised to measure the result at twelve months by asking the people who used it, and forbade collecting the details that would take. Both clauses were written by the same person on the same afternoon.
- It said the answer would be about *this model*, while the list of what gets collected stops at the make. One word, two sections apart.
- A currency mentioned once, in a throwaway example, set the currency of every screen in the finished thing. **An example is a specification whether you meant it as one or not.**

None of that was visible while the brief sat on a page.
