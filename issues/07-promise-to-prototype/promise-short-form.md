# The promise, short form

**One page. This is the filter everything went through.**

If you want to produce one of these for your own promise, the [full interview](promise.skill.md) takes about twenty-five minutes. This page is what came out of it, and it is the shape to copy.

---

## The sentence

> ### "I know whether it is worth fixing, instead of guessing."

Said by a householder standing in a kitchen with a repairer's quote in their hand.

**It passes the spoken test** - a person would say this out loud, to another person.
**It passes the vocabulary test** - it contains no noun from inside the business.

A promise is the change in the customer's situation that sits between their problem and your solution. It is in their words, it is true or false by a date, and they could tell which without being told.

---

## The six fields

A commitment with no date, no scope and no instrument is not a commitment. It is a mood.

| # | Field | Status | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Horizon** | **YES** | 15 September 2026, and September 2027 |
| 2 | **Scope** | **YES** | One category, five faults, three makes |
| 3 | **Instrument** | **YES** | Second-quote rate · twelve-month survival · share of jobs finishing at or under the walk-away line |
| 4 | **Party** | **YES** | Any reader who runs the five faults and tries to break it |
| 5 | **Baseline** | **NO** | Nobody measured what people do today |
| 6 | **Dependency** | **YES** | Narrowed until it stood on records we control |

### Five out of six.

**The missing one is published as missing.** The instruments are all comparisons and nobody took the "before" reading, so the September 2027 horizon cannot be settled as things stand. It is recoverable - it needs measuring, not building - and it was not done.

## What it is never allowed to do

The six must-nots. The last three are the ones that cost money.

1. **Never recommend repair on a safety-related fault.** Safety exits the tool immediately, before any arithmetic.
2. **Never present a confident answer where the data is thin.** Thin data is stated as thin.
3. **Never rank or weight an answer by who pays commission on the outcome.**
4. **Never present a repairer's judgement as an outcome.** An unlogged decline is a missing record, not a machine that did not exist.
5. **Never collect more than the answer needs.** Make, age, symptoms, quote, decision. No account, no address, no email.
6. **Never use paid or biased opinions from manufacturers or repairers. Records only.**

**On (6), because it is the one that does the most work.** A manufacturer's stated service life, a repairer's *"these last about eight years"*, a trade-body lifespan table - all are somebody's claim about their own product and all fail the same test.

> **What happened is admissible. What someone says usually happens is not.**

It deletes the easy shortcut. The obvious way to fill a thin dataset is to seed it with published average-lifespan figures, and this forbids exactly that - so the tool has less to say on day one and has to admit it, which is rule 2 doing its job at the moment it is most expensive.
