# The promise

**What this is for.** You are about to have something built. Before you brief anyone or anything, this turns what you know into a promise a builder can work from and you can check.

**Twenty-five minutes cold, or about ten if you bring your documents.** It stops as soon as the promise can answer the questions a build will ask. It does not ask you fifty questions.

**What it will not do.** It will not tell you what to build, rank your ideas, or write your specification. It makes one promise you have already decided to make specific enough to be built and checked.

---

## HOW TO RUN THIS

Paste this whole file into any assistant and say: **"Run this on my promise."** Then answer as it asks. Answer short. If you do not know, say you do not know - that is a finding and it gets recorded.

**Assistant: follow the sections in order. Ask ONE question at a time and wait. Do not skip ahead, do not summarise back at length, and do not offer solutions at any point. If the user gives you a long answer, take it and move on.**

### BRING YOUR DOCUMENTS - optional, and it shortens the interview

**The promise is not the beginning of anything.** Strategy, business model, operating model and roadmap come first, and most of what this interview asks was decided in those. **Paste or attach them and the interview gets shorter.**

Useful: the strategy or business-model document · the operating model · the roadmap or initiative list · customer research, interview notes, support or complaint data · anything with the current numbers in it.

**Assistant, when documents are supplied:**
1. Read them and fill in every field you can. **Show the user what you filled in, say which document each came from, and ask them to confirm or correct.** Confirming is faster than answering.
2. **Then ask only the questions that are still unanswered.** A well-documented promise can come out of this in six questions rather than eighteen.
3. **Cite the source next to each field in the final brief** - which document, which section. Somebody will ask where a line came from.

⚠ **ONE HARD EXCEPTION, and it matters more than the rest of this section.**

**Never write the promise sentence from a document.** Q1 must be spoken by a person. **A strategy deck is the richest possible source of internal language** - it is written in system names, capability names and programme names, which is exactly what the vocabulary test exists to catch. A promise lifted from a deck will pass a reading and fail a customer.

**You may say: "your document says X. What would the customer have said?" You may not lift it.**

### FIVE QUESTIONS ARE ALWAYS ASKED, whatever the documents say

These cannot be inferred from anything, because their value is in the answer being said out loud:

- **G1 and G2** - provenance. No document tells you honestly where a promise came from.
- **Q1** - the promise, in the customer's words. See above.
- **Q7 and Q8** - what you reached for first, and what the promise needs. **The value is the gap between them. Infer it from a document and the gap disappears.**
- **Q11** - what must never be true. Strategy documents state ambitions, not prohibitions.
- **Q17** - what you depend on and do not control. Documents never say this.

**Everything else may be inferred and confirmed.**

### HOW DEEP TO GO

**Assistant: at most ONE follow-up per question**, and only when the answer does not satisfy the field it belongs to. Then move on. **A tool that keeps digging is a tool nobody finishes**, and an incomplete promise honestly marked beats a complete one abandoned at question thirty.

**The user may say "push me on this" at any point.** Then go as deep as they want on that question only, and return to the sequence afterwards.

**The user may add their own questions.** Take them, ask them at the end, and put the answers in the brief under **ALSO ASKED** so they are visibly theirs and not part of the standard set.

## GATE - where this comes from

**Ask these two first. Do not proceed to Part 1 until both are answered.**

**G1.** Where does this promise come from? Name the specific contact with customers behind it - interviews, service calls, complaints, a sales conversation, watching someone use the thing.

**G2.** When, and with how many?

**Then say back, in one line, which of these it is:**

- **HEARD** - it came from customers saying it, and you can say when and how many.
- **INFERRED** - it came from data about customers rather than from customers.
- **ASSUMED** - it came from inside the building.

**Do not stop the interview if the answer is ASSUMED.** Record it, print it at the top of the output, and carry on. A promise that says *assumed* at the top is honest. A promise that has been through a structure and does not say where it came from is laundered, and this tool exists partly to stop that.

---

## PART 1 - the promise, in the customer's words

**Q1.** In one sentence, what does the customer get that they do not get today? **Write it as something the customer would say, not as something you would say about them.**

**Q2.** Who is "the customer" here, precisely? A role and a situation, not a segment.

**Q3.** What do they do today instead? What does that cost them - in time, money, or their own effort?

**Q4.** What have they told you they want that you are deliberately NOT promising here?

### Then run both edge tests on the sentence from Q1. Both, every time.

**THE SPOKEN TEST - is it too loose?**
> **Would this customer have actually said this sentence, out loud, to a person?**

Read it back and ask. If it contains *better*, *seamless*, *improved*, *optimised*, *more efficient* or *world-class*, no real person said it. Send it back and ask for what they would have said instead. Real customer sentences are concrete and slightly awkward: *I found out on the day, not before.* *I had to phone you twice.* *I ordered the wrong one.*

**THE VOCABULARY TEST - is it too tight?**
> **Does the sentence contain a noun from inside your organisation?**

A system name, a table, a screen, a field, a process step, a piece of your architecture. **If it does, it is an instruction wearing the customer's voice.** Customers cannot name your internals because they do not know they exist. Cut the noun and ask what the customer would have noticed instead.

**Do not move on until the sentence passes both.** Show the user the final sentence and confirm it before continuing.

---

## PART 2 - what has to change for it to be true

**Q5.** For this promise to hold, what has to happen that does not happen today? Describe the change in the customer's experience, not the mechanism.

**Q6.** Which of these does the promise actually move? Pick one, not several.
- **Sooner** - they know something earlier than they do now
- **Wider** - they are offered something they would have had to ask for
- **Without you** - something happens that used to need one of your people
- **Cheaper** - it costs them less, in money or their own effort

**If the user picks more than one, ask which one, if it were the only one delivered, would still make the promise worth making.** That is the answer.

**Q7.** Before you think about it: what kind of help would you have reached for first?
- **Predict** - it tells you something before you would have known it
- **Generate** - it produces material: options, drafts, variants, copy
- **Act** - it does the step, and the step stops waiting for a person
- **Apply** - it runs on your own material, so the answer is about you rather than about the world

**Q8.** Now: which of those four does the promise actually need? Take your time on this one.

**If Q7 and Q8 differ, say so plainly and record both.** That difference is the most valuable thing this interview produces. Most work is funded as the answer to Q7 and needed the answer to Q8, and nobody notices because the thing that gets built is competent and finished and answers a question nobody asked.

**Q9.** What is the smallest version of this that would still be worth having?

---

## PART 3 - what must not happen

**This part is what catches drift later. Do not shorten it.**

**Q10.** What would make this worse than doing nothing?

**Q11.** What must never be true, even if everything else works? Rules, limits, things that must not be shown, said, changed or automated.

**Q12.** Who else touches this, and what would break for them if it worked?

---

## PART 4 - how you will know

**Q13.** What would the customer accept as proof that the promise moved? **Something they would recognise as a change in their own situation, without being told what it means.** Not an internal metric.

**Q14.** Is that a comparison? If yes: what is the number today, and where does it come from?

**If they do not have today's number, say this plainly: a comparison with no baseline is a recollection. Ask whether they can get it before the build starts. If not, record it as missing.**

**Q15.** By what date will it be checked?

**Q16.** Who checks it, by name or role?

---

## PART 5 - what you do not control

**Q17.** What does this promise depend on that somebody else owns? Data you do not hold, a system you do not run, a decision another party has to make, a supply you cannot guarantee.

**Then ask: what is the plan if they say no?**

**If there is no plan, say this and do not soften it: this promise has an uncontrolled dependency. It is not a risk to manage later. It is the thing that decides whether the promise exists.** The usual answer is to narrow the promise until the dependency is one you control - a smaller scope built on material you already have. That is a better outcome than a large promise resting on somebody else's goodwill.

---

## PART 6 - the room

**Q18.** What are you deliberately leaving open for whoever builds this?

**If the answer is "nothing", push once.** A promise that specifies everything is an implementation written in prose, and it buys you nothing but typing. The space you leave is where a builder can bring something you would not have thought of. **Name at least two decisions you are happy for them to make.**

---

## THE SCORE

**Now score the promise on five fields. For each, answer YES or NOT YET, and say why in one line. Do not soften this.**

| # | Field | It is YES when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Horizon** | There is a date by which the promise either held or did not |
| 2 | **Scope** | It is clear who this is for and who it is not for |
| 3 | **Instrument** | Something exists that could show the promise was broken |
| 4 | **Party** | A named person or role is accountable for checking |
| 5 | **Baseline** | Today's number is written down, not remembered |
| 6 | **Dependency** | Everything the promise needs is either controlled by you, or there is a stated plan for getting it |

**Then print:**

> **N of 6 fields satisfied. The ones that are NOT YET are not gaps in this document. They are the parts of the promise that are still a mood.**

**The six are not equal, and one of them can stop the build on its own.**

- **A missing BASELINE is recoverable.** Go and measure it before you start. Say so and carry on.
- **A failing DEPENDENCY is a veto.** Whatever the total score, if the promise rests on something another party owns and there is no plan for getting it, print: **do not build this yet. Narrow the promise until it stands on what you control, and run this again.**

**A promise scoring 3 or less overall should not be handed to a builder either. Say so.**

---

## THE HANDOVER

**Finally, write out the document below and nothing else. This is what gets pasted to whoever or whatever builds it.**

```
PROMISE BRIEF

PROVENANCE:   [HEARD / INFERRED / ASSUMED] - [G1 and G2, one line]
BUILT FROM:   [documents used, or "interview only". Note next to any field that came from
              a document rather than from a person.]
SCORE:        [N] of 5 fields. Not yet: [list]

THE PROMISE, IN THE CUSTOMER'S WORDS
[Q1, verbatim, after both edge tests]

WHO
[Q2 - role and situation]

WHAT THEY DO TODAY, AND WHAT IT COSTS THEM
[Q3]

WHAT MUST BECOME TRUE
[Q5. What changes for the customer. Not how.]

WHICH KIND OF CHANGE THIS IS
[Q6 - one of: sooner / wider / without you / cheaper]

THE MODE THIS PROMISE NEEDS
Reached for first: [Q7]   Actually needed: [Q8]
[If these differ, say so here in one line. It is the most useful sentence in the brief.]

SMALLEST VERSION WORTH HAVING
[Q9]

WHAT MUST NOT HAPPEN
[Q10, Q11, Q12 as a list. Treat every line here as a hard constraint.]

HOW WE WILL KNOW
Evidence the customer would accept: [Q13]
Baseline today:                     [Q14, or MISSING]
Checked by:                         [Q15, Q16]

DEPENDS ON, AND WE DO NOT CONTROL
[Q17, with the plan. If there is no plan, write NO PLAN and the build does not start.]

DELIBERATELY NOT SPECIFIED
[Q18. These decisions are yours to make. Make them and say what you decided.]

NOT PROMISED HERE
[Q4]

ALSO ASKED
[Any questions the user added, with their answers. Empty if none.]
```

---

## AFTER THE BUILD

**Keep the brief open while it is built. Every time a decision gets made that the brief did not answer, write it down in one of two columns:**

- **In the room** - it falls under DEPENDS ON, AND WE DO NOT CONTROL
[Q17, with the plan. If there is no plan, write NO PLAN and the build does not start.]

DELIBERATELY NOT SPECIFIED. That is the promise working.
- **Drift** - it does not, and it changes what the customer gets.

**Count both at the end.** A good promise produces some of the first and almost none of the second. **If you have no entries in the first column, the promise was too tight and you paid for a typist.**
